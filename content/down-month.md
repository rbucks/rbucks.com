Title: What a Down Month Feels Like
Date: 2026-05-13 09:00
Slug: down-month
Category: Business
Tags: entrepreneurship, shovels, startups, growth, leadership
Description: What it actually feels like to run a startup through a down month — the dull ache, the calm of resilience, and the refusal to sail small.
Status: draft

I can tell you what it feels like to have a down month. It starts slow, a dull ache in the chest. It's not the kind of pressure that makes it hard to breathe; it's just there. It sits with you. Even when you're not thinking about the business, it's a constant buzz, like my neighbor's leaf blower.

At this stage in the business we are no longer fragile. I mean we can last forever… if that's the plan. We achieved that mythical escape velocity some time ago. If we had to cut back to a skeleton crew and sail this ship, we could do it with no outside help. This thought produces a calm. The leaf blower cuts off.

An easy sail on calm seas with a small crew is not what we intended, though. We're here to win the America's Cup, be the best of the best, and that requires resources, speed, and execution. It means making up for losses. A bad month means the next one better be twice as good! The dull ache is knowing that the work isn't stopping. A good month isn't good enough. A bad month means work twice as hard!

Yes, I'm pumping myself up, but I'm also sharing this because you should know that this is the plan. We're not aspiring to be small. We're not trying to be a nice, little profitable tech company. The scope of making public data publicly accessible is too broad, too overwhelming, and too fun to pass up.
